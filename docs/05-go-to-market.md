# 05 — Go-to-Market

The strategy mirrors the overall Spendy plan: start where the founders can reach
retailers and students in the same place — **university precincts** — and use the
small-retailer product to "force" a reason to download the app.

## Primary channel — university-precinct cafes

- **Target:** cafes, coffee shops, and casual eateries around UNSW first, where
  the founders have reach and the customer base (students) is app-native.
- **Pitch to the retailer:** "Issue your first 10 gift cards free. No app to
  build, no POS to change. We help your customers actually come back and spend."
- **On-the-ground acquisition of consumers:**
  - QR code on the counter and on the back of issued gift cards.
  - QR / claim link on receipts.
  - Word of mouth — students who like the app keep using it and refer others.

## Secondary channels

- Referral giveaways (reward users who refer a friend who downloads).
- Social media.
- Shopping-centre interview videos and events (e.g. with mums).
- Corporate gift card sponsorships of events.

## Pilot plan

- **Cohort:** 5–10 cafes near UNSW.
- **Window:** 6–8 weeks.
- **Measured against the metrics in [03 — MVP](03-mvp.md):** cards issued per
  retailer/month, claim rate, redemption rate vs the ~19% national expiry/loss
  rate, % of redeemers spending above card value, and app installs attributable
  to claims.
- **Goal:** prove the loop end-to-end (issue → claim → redeem) and show retailers
  a retention lift they can feel, then use those results as the reference case
  for the next wave of retailers.

## Why this sequencing works

Each onboarded cafe is a small, self-contained test of the whole flywheel: the
retailer gets a retention tool, and every card they issue is a QR pointed at a
new Spendy consumer. Starting narrow (one precinct, one retailer type) keeps the
pilot measurable before widening.
